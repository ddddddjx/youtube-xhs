CMU Opens Access to Top Computer Science Classes Through Free CS Learn Platform

The 
Kenneth C. Griffin School of Computer Science
 at Carnegie Mellon University has launched 
CS Learn
, a new online learning platform that offers free access to select courses from Carnegie Mellon’s 
top-ranked
 computer science program. 
CS Learn makes courses taught by leading experts in their fields available on YouTube and the CS Learn website. The platform will feature a handful of lectures from some of the Griffin School’s most popular courses, with more to be added as CS Learn grows. 
“We’re able to share the teaching expertise of people on the frontier of their subjects, and the cutting edge of the interdisciplinary nature of our school,” said 
Phillip Compeau
, a teaching professor in the 
Ray and Stephanie Lane Computational Biology Department
 and assistant dean for innovation in computing education. "We have an entire department dedicated to computational biology, and another to human-computer interaction. We have something special to offer."
The first courses available on CS Learn include Principles of Computing, Generative AI, AI Agents and Machine Learning in Production, and Software Architectures. The lineup will expand greatly in the spring and beyond, in terms of both the number of courses offered and the type of content available. 
Computer science as a field is constantly evolving, and the Griffin School’s educational offerings are responding. CS Learn offers interested learners around the world an opportunity to participate in the groundbreaking education happening in Pittsburgh.
CS Learn will keep pace with the rapid changes and advancements in computer science, and its courses will give learners the latest in computer science education. CS Learn’s unique question-and-answer feature allows courses to retain the actual questions asked by students in the classroom and the answers from faculty. This course element puts viewers in the discussions happening within the Griffin School while protecting student privacy.
The idea for CS Learn and expanding educational opportunities through online courses started years ago for Compeau, who pitched the idea for CS Learn to a class in his cohort while earning his MBA at CMU's 
Tepper School of Business
. The aim, he said, was to expand the reach of the Griffin School through these courses, and the response from his cohort was overwhelmingly positive. From the initial idea to launch, CS Learn has been a CMU project that relies on the interdisciplinary nature of the Griffin School. 
CS Learn is built around courses the Griffin School was already recording through the 
Software and Societal Systems Department
's videography team. The team records some of the school’s most popular courses, but until now they weren’t available outside CMU.
ScottyLabs
, a student-run organization that creates technological solutions for CMU, helped make the recorded videos ready for broad distribution. The students built an AI-assisted system that turned the raw classroom recordings from the videography team into videos optimized for online learning. The videos focus on the professor and any slides shown during the course. Any visible student faces are blurred to protect privacy. If a student asks a question, the audio is cut to further protect privacy, but the question appears as on-screen text so online learners can follow along. 
The AI-assisted system allows courses to quickly move from the CMU classroom to CS Learn, so online learners can follow what CMU students are learning at the frontier of computer science this semester, within weeks of the original lecture.
“We're taking this recording of a course that is cutting edge or on a topic that largely isn't taught elsewhere, and we'll make the recording available for free,” Compeau said. “That will take us to places that we hadn’t even considered and put us in front of people who never otherwise would have had an opportunity to come to CMU and do this.”
