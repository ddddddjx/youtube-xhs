# Terracotta TikTok v3: independent review

File: `成片/兵马俑_TikTok_v3.mp4` (60.0 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps, music track, no VO)
Method: frames every 0.5 s plus full-res stills at 0.0 / 2.8 / 3.2 / 8.3 / 8.8 / 13.8 / 28.4 / 31.8 / 33.0 / 36.5 / 44.6 / 59.5 s, with safe-area guides at 75% height and 88% width. ffmpeg found no hard scene cuts: it is one continuous camera move. No black frames.

## Score: 6/10

The concept and art direction are good: a painted warrior that decays to gray, then a halftone evidence wall, index cards that type themselves, red string. The facts hold up (1974, Pit 1 at 230 m, 8,000+ figures, about 1 mile, Sima Qian's mercury and crossbows, the 2020 mercury study, 20+ sq mi). Execution falls short in three places. About a third of the runtime (10 to 29 s) is a beige diagram with thin lines that reads like a slideshow. Red stamps and labels keep landing under the captions. The ending pull-out is too small to read. The grandeur of the army barely appears: the only real photo of the army in color is on screen for 2 s.

## 1. Hook (0 to 3 s)
- **Works:** frame 0 is a full-bleed, color-painted warrior face with "They weren't always gray." on screen from the first frame. The paint peeling away (1.0 to 2.0 s) is a real visual event.
- **Problems:**
  - The painted version looks like a flat digital tint. The teal scarf is a single solid fill and the skin is an orange gradient. US viewers will read it as a filter, not a reconstruction. Without a "how it looked in 210 BC" tag, it does not land as a revelation.
  - 2.0 to 3.0 s holds a gray head with nothing moving except confetti flecks. That is dead time inside the hook window.
  - Nothing in the first 3 s says what this is or why it matters. No "2,200 years", no "8,000 soldiers", no "the tomb nobody will open". "They weren't always gray" is a curiosity line about paint, and the video then drops the paint thread until 51 s. The strongest hook (a sealed tomb with rivers of mercury that China refuses to open) is buried at 44 to 56 s.

## 2. Captions: readability and safe area
- The English and Chinese captions sit above the bottom 25% and inside the right 12% in every frame. Size is good.
- **Captions on the beige paper (10 to 29 s, 29 to 34 s):** the yellow/white fill with a thick black outline on light beige looks muddy. The outline eats the word gaps, so lines read like "Pit1aloneis755feet". On dark backgrounds the same style is fine.
- **Transition ghosting:** at 5.5, 13.0, 19.5, 22.0, 27.0, 29.0 and 32.0 s the outgoing and incoming captions cross-fade with blur, producing a doubled, gray, unreadable caption for about 0.3 s. Use a hard cut or a quick pop.
- 44.5 s: the three-line English caption plus the Chinese line ("史书记载：里面有水银江河，还有会自动发射的弩") is the longest block. The Chinese line runs almost edge to edge (x ≈ 15 to 1030), past the right safe line. Shorten it or wrap it.
- 59 to 60 s: "Would you open it?" sits directly on top of the miniature wall (the cross-section card and the face grid). It is legible, but it covers the content the pull-out is meant to reveal.

## 4. Does each shot show what the caption says?
- **"1974. Farmers in China dig a well." (3 to 5.5 s):** no farmers and no well. The shot is a warrior headshot plus a pit photo. Any archival-style photo of a well, a shovel or a field would fix it.
- **"Then they hit a face." (5.5 to 8 s):** the red hand-drawn circle is on a figure in the pit photo. But the photo is a heavy halftone of torsos and heads, and the circled object reads as a blob. At phone size you cannot tell it is a face. Zoom in further and use less halftone in the circled area.
- **"Pit 1 alone is 755 feet long." / "two football fields" (10 to 16 s):** the pit rectangle with its dot grid works. The two football fields are plain rectangles with vertical lines, sized about equal to the pit, and their label is hidden (see above). Add end zones and green fill, or one goalpost icon, and move the label above the fields.
- **16.0 to 16.5 s:** the camera whips back to the halftone army photo while the caption still says "football fields". That is a mismatch.
- **"Thousands of soldiers, in battle formation" (17 to 19 s):** this is the best shot in the film, a real color photo of Pit 1 under the hangar. It lasts only 2 s, and the soldiers are tiny at the bottom, with the roof trusses taking the upper half.
- **Pits 2, 3, 4 and "faces east" (19 to 29 s):** the schematics are legible, but the three red/gray strings run diagonally straight through the map in every frame, crossing Pit 1 and the Pit 4 "?" mark. It looks like a rendering bug, not design.
- **"…guarding one man's tomb, a mile away." (29 to 31.5 s):** the site plan with the "1 mile" dashed line works, but the tomb square and the pit icons are tiny and on the right edge (in the UI zone) at 29.0 s.
- **"Qin Shi Huang. The First Emperor of China." (32 to 35 s):** the picture is a photo of trees on a hill. There is no emperor (no statue, portrait or seal) and the "ONE MAN" stamp is hidden. This is the protagonist reveal and it is the weakest image in the film.
- **"Every face is different" (35 to 41 s):** a good beat, but about 4 of the 9 tiles are so washed out or blurred by halftone that they read as gray noise (top-left, top-right, center, bottom-right). Several are also different angles of the same 2 or 3 heads. "No two alike" is undermined if viewers cannot see the faces. Use 9 distinct, high-contrast frontal faces.
- **"Scans found a hidden chamber about 100 feet underground" (41 to 44.5 s):** the cross-section with "~100 ft" and the "hidden chamber" box is clear. The "~100 ft" bracket sits at the right edge, near the safe line.
- **Mercury and crossbows (44.5 to 48 s):** the quote card with yellow highlights ("mercury was made into rivers and seas…", "crossbows set to shoot anyone who comes near") is legible. Good. But **crossbows are never drawn**, and the card's last line types in as the caption is already leaving.
- **"Today, mercury above the tomb is way higher than normal." (48 to 51 s):** the Hg card works, but its key words ("far more mercury than normal") finish typing only about 0.5 s before the camera leaves.
- **"Last time they dug, the colors died in minutes." (51 to 53.5 s):** this is the best callback to the opening, but it shows a close-up of armor with red paint remnants. Bring back the color-to-gray face from 0 s instead. That is the hook paying off.
- **"So his tomb stays sealed. For now." (53.5 to 56 s):** the SEALED stamp on the mound photo is strong and readable.

## 5. Empty frames, jumps, tiny content, slideshow feel
- **9.0 to 10.0 s: an effectively empty frame.** Plain beige grid plus two lines, with no subject, for a full second while the camera travels. Viewers will swipe here.
- **10 to 29 s: about 19 s on a flat beige diagram** with thin lines and small black tag labels. Contrast and motion drop sharply after the dark, textured wall. This stretch reads as a slideshow of schematics and is the biggest retention risk.
- **The zoom jumps at 16.0 → 17.0 and 18.5 → 19.0 s** (photo → beige map) are abrupt changes of world, not continuous moves.
- **The final pull-out (56.5 to 60 s) is too small.** The whole wall is a narrow vertical strip about 30% of the frame width, centered, with black on both sides. Every card is unreadable at phone size and the frame is static for the last 2.5 s (57.5 to 60). The "everything connects" payoff does not come across: no string lights up and nothing is highlighted. Either pull out less (fill at least 80% of the width) or animate one final string from the tomb to the camera.
- Audio peaks at 0.0 dBFS (mean −21.9 dB). Possible clipping, so normalize to about −1 dBTP.

## 6. Grandeur and mystery
Mystery: **medium.** Mercury rivers, crossbow traps, a sealed tomb and "Would you open it?" form a strong arc. They arrive in the last 16 s.
Grandeur: **weak.** The scale of 8,000 soldiers is shown as a dot grid and a 9-photo grid. The one real wide shot of the pit gets 2 s and is cropped to the roof. The emperor gets a picture of trees. Nothing feels epic: no slow push across rows of warriors, and no low-angle hero shot.

## Prioritized fix list
1. **10 to 29 s, the beige diagram stretch:** cut it to about 10 s and keep the dark board. Put the pit schematics on a dark index card pinned to the wall, and intercut a real wide photo of the rows of warriors (a slow push-in) between "755 feet" and "battle formation". Hold the Pit 1 color photo for 3 to 4 s and crop it to the soldiers, not the roof.
2. **Text that sits under captions:** move or time "AN ENTIRE ARMY" (8.3 s), "2 FOOTBALL FIELDS" (13.5 s) and "ONE MAN" (31.5 s) so each sits at least 150 px above the caption block. Rule: no stamp or label in y 1250 to 1500.
3. **Hook (0 to 3 s):** add a small tag "210 BC, original paint" on the color face. Make the painted texture real (multiply the color over the photo, no flat fills). Cut the 2.0 to 3.0 s gray hold, or put a second hook line there, e.g. "8,000 of them guard a tomb no one will open." Consider bringing the sealed-tomb question to the front.
4. **The final pull-out (56.5 to 60 s):** stop at a scale where the cards fill at least 80% of the width, highlight the string path (face → pit → tomb → mercury → SEALED), and keep something moving to the last frame. Move "Would you open it?" off the content or onto a dark plate.
5. **Half-typed labels:** every typewriter label must finish at least 0.8 s before the camera leaves: 3.0 to 5.5 s (date card), 17.0 s (PIT 1 · TODAY), 27.5 to 28.5 s (ALL FACING EAST), 44.5 s (mercury rivers), 48 to 50.5 s (Hg card). Shorten the text or start typing earlier.
6. **32 to 35 s, emperor reveal:** replace the tree photo with a halftone of the Qin Shi Huang statue or portrait, and stamp "ONE MAN" over it, above the caption.
7. **35 to 41 s, face grid:** swap the 4 muddy tiles for distinct, high-contrast frontal faces. Lower the halftone strength on the faces.
8. **9.0 to 10.0 s, empty beige frame:** shorten the travel to about 0.3 s, or keep a visible subject (the string end, a pin) in frame the whole way.
9. **19 to 29 s, strings through the pit map:** route the three strings to end at the map's edge, not through Pit 1 and Pit 4. Pull "PIT 2 · CAVALRY + CHARIOTS" and the Pit 2 outline in to x ≤ 950.
10. **Caption style on light backgrounds:** on beige, drop the thick outline and use a dark semi-opaque plate behind the text. Add about 2% tracking so word gaps survive.
11. **Caption transitions:** replace the blur cross-fades (5.5, 13.0, 19.5, 22.0, 27.0, 29.0, 32.0 s) with hard swaps or a 4-frame pop.
12. **Small fixes:** show a well or farmers at 3 to 5 s. Zoom tighter on the circled face at 6 to 8 s. Draw a crossbow at 46 s. Use the color-to-gray face as the callback at 51 s. Wrap the Chinese caption at 44.5 s. Normalize audio to −1 dBTP.
